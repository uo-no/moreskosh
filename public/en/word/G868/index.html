Senses (NGSD)

  1. to depart / go away (8)
    To leave, depart, withdraw: “depart from me”, “the devil departed from Jesus”, “that it might leave me”, “the angel left him”, “did not leave the temple”, “withdrew from the disciples”.
    Attested: NT · LXX · Fathers

    • Luke 2:37

      and she had been a widow for about eighty-four years), who didn’t depart from the temple, worshiping with fastings and petitions night and day.

  3. to lead into revolt (transitive) (1)
    To make people break away, to win them over: “drew away people after him in revolt”.
    Attested: NT only

Abbott-Smith dictionary entry (quoted)

14 ἀφ-ίστημι, [in LXX for סור, מעל, etc. (41 words in all);] trans. in pres., impf., fut., 1 aor., to put away, lead away; metaph., to move to revolt: Ac 5:37. Intrans. in pf., plpf., 2 aor., to stand off, depart from, withdraw from: c. gen., Lk 2:37; seq. ἀπό, Lk 4:13 13:27, Ac 5:38 12:10 15:38 19:9 22:29, II Co 12:8; metaph., ἀπὸ ἀδικίας, II Ti 2:19; ἀπὸ θεοῦ, (fall away, apostatize), He 3:12. Mid. (exc. 1 aor., wh. is trans.), to withdraw oneself from, absent oneself from: Lk 2:37; metaph., fall away, apostatize: absol., Lk 8:13; c. gen., I Ti 4:1 (MM, s.v.; Cremer, 308).†
