Senses (IGSD)

  1. into (entering) (542)
    Movement into a place or container. Also being thrown or falling in.
  2. to (going / arriving) (434)
    Direction toward a destination and arrival. Also spreading among people, and turning the eyes toward.
  3. on / upon (contact) (25)
    Contact with a surface. Striking the cheek, spitting on, spreading on the road.
  4. in / at (location) (108)
    Being or staying at a place as the result of movement. In later Greek it can stand in for ἐν.
  5. toward (a person) (78)
    The direction of an attitude or act toward a person: sin, love, anger, kindness. Also serves as the basis of an oath.
  6. for / as (purpose / use) (265)
    For purpose, use, or recipient. "As a testimony," "as a memorial," the purpose of εἰς τό + infinitive, for repentance and forgiveness.
  7. until / for (time) (66)
    The limit and length of time. "Forever," "until the end," "until the next day," "for generations."
  8. into (faith / baptism) (71)
    The form that leads to the object of πιστεύω (believe) and βαπτίζω (baptize). "To believe in Christ" expresses entrusting oneself to Christ and being united with him; likewise being united to the name or to Christ in baptism.
  9. into / to (result / change) (170)
    The end result of a change or act. Includes being divided in two, coming to ruin or judgment, being counted as righteous.

2112 εἰς, prep. c. acc., expressing entrance, direction, limit, into, unto, to, upon, towards, for, among (Lat. in, c. acc.). Of place. After verbs of motion; of entrance into: Mt 8:23 9:7, Mk 1:45, Lk 2:15 8:31, al.; of approach, to or towards: Mk 11:1, Lk 6:8 19:28, Jo 11:31 21:6, al.; before pl. and collective nouns, among: Mk 4:7 8:19, 20, Lk 11:49, Jo 21:23, al.; Of a limit reached, unto, on, upon: Mt 8:18, 21:1, Mk 11:1 13:16, Lk 14:10, Jo 6:3 11:32, al.; c. acc. pers. (as in Ep. and Ion.), Ac 23:15, Ro 5:12 16:19, II Co 10:14; elliptical: ἐπιστολαὶ εἰς Δαμασκόν, Ac 9:2; ἡ διακονία μου ἡ εἰς Ἱ., Ro 15:31; metaph., of entrance into a certain state or condition, or of approach or direction towards some end (Thayer, B, i, 1; ii, 1), εἰς τ. ὄνομα, M, Pr., 200. Of relation, to, towards, for, in regard to (so in cl., but more freq. in late Gk., εἰς encroaching on the simple dat., which it has wholly displaced in MGr.; Jannaris, Gr., §1541; Robertson, Gr., 594; Deiss., BS, 117f.): Lk 7:30, Ro 4:20 15:2, 26, I Co 16:1, Eph 3:16, al.; ἀγάπη εἰς, Ro 5:8, al.; χρηστός, Eph 4:32; φρονεῖν εἰς, Ro 12:16; θαρρεῖν, II Co 10:1.
