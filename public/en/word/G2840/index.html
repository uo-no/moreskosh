Senses (NGSD)

  1. to defile / make unclean (12)
    To defile ritually, to make unclean: “what comes out of the mouth defiles a person”, “what goes into the mouth does not defile”, “has defiled this holy place”, “those who are defiled”.
    Attested: NT only

    • Matthew 15:11

      οὐ τὸ εἰσερχόμενον εἰς τὸ στόμα κοινοῖ τὸν ἄνθρωπον, ἀλλὰ τὸ ἐκπορευόμενον ἐκ τοῦ στόματος τοῦτο κοινοῖ τὸν ἄνθρωπον.

      That which enters into the mouth doesn’t defile the man; but that which proceeds out of the mouth, this defiles the man.”

      Read Matthew 15:11 in the app

    • Matthew 15:20

      ταῦτά ἐστιν τὰ κοινοῦντα τὸν ἄνθρωπον, τὸ δὲ ἀνίπτοις χερσὶν φαγεῖν οὐ κοινοῖ τὸν ἄνθρωπον.

      These are the things which defile the man; but to eat with unwashed hands doesn’t defile the man.”

      Read Matthew 15:20 in the app

    • Mark 7:20

      ἔλεγεν δὲ ὅτι Τὸ ἐκ τοῦ ἀνθρώπου ἐκπορευόμενον ἐκεῖνο κοινοῖ τὸν ἄνθρωπον·

      He said, “That which proceeds out of the man, that defiles the man.

      Read Mark 7:20 in the app

  2. to count as unclean (2)
    To declare or regard as “unclean”: “What God has made clean, do not call unclean.”
    Attested: NT only

    • Acts 10:15

      καὶ φωνὴ πάλιν ἐκ δευτέρου πρὸς αὐτόν· Ἃ ὁ θεὸς ἐκαθάρισεν σὺ μὴ κοίνου.

      A voice came to him again the second time, “What God has cleansed, you must not call unclean.”

      Read Acts 10:15 in the app

    • Acts 11:9

      ἀπεκρίθη δὲ φωνὴ ἐκ δευτέρου ἐκ τοῦ οὐρανοῦ· Ἃ ὁ θεὸς ἐκαθάρισεν σὺ μὴ κοίνου.

      But a voice answered me the second time out of heaven, ‘What God has cleansed, don’t you call unclean.’

Abbott-Smith dictionary entry (quoted)

14 κοινόω, -ῶ, [in LXX: IV Mac 7:6 אR*;] in cl., to make common. In LXX, IV Mac 7:6, and NT (as κοινός, 2), to make ceremonially unclean, to profane (= cl., βεληλόω): Mt 15:11, 18, 20, Mk 7:15, 18, 20, 23, Ac 21:28; pass., He 9:13. to count unclean (cf. δικαιόω): Ac 10:15 11:9.† SYN.: βεβηλόω. Thayer (s.v. κ.) mentions that Winer notes the accuracy whereby the Jews are said to use κ. in addressing Jews, Ac 21:28, and β. when speaking to Felix, 24:6 (Cremer, 362).
