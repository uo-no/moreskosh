Senses (NGSD)

  1. to get, to acquire (6)
    To get, to acquire, to buy: “do not take gold, silver or copper in your purses”, “I give a tenth of all that I get”, “to obtain the gift of God with money”, “he acquired a field with the reward of his wrongdoing”, “I bought this citizenship for a large sum”, “by your endurance you will gain your lives”.
    Attested: NT · LXX · Fathers

    • Matthew 10:9

      μὴ κτήσησθε χρυσὸν μηδὲ ἄργυρον μηδὲ χαλκὸν εἰς τὰς ζώνας ὑμῶν,

      Don’t take any gold, silver, or brass in your money belts.

      Read Matthew 10:9 in the app

    • Luke 21:19

      ἐν τῇ ὑπομονῇ ὑμῶν κτήσασθε τὰς ψυχὰς ὑμῶν.

      “By your endurance you will win your lives.

      Read Luke 21:19 in the app

    • Acts 8:20

      Πέτρος δὲ εἶπεν πρὸς αὐτόν· Τὸ ἀργύριόν σου σὺν σοὶ εἴη εἰς ἀπώλειαν ὅτι τὴν δωρεὰν τοῦ θεοῦ ἐνόμισας διὰ χρημάτων κτᾶσθαι.

      But Peter said to him, “May your silver perish with you, because you thought you could obtain the gift of God with money!

      Read Acts 8:20 in the app

  2. to make one’s own, to control (disputed) (1)
    Keeping “one’s own vessel” (σκεῦος read either as the body or as a wife).
    Attested: NT only

    • 1 Thessalonians 4:4

      εἰδέναι ἕκαστον ὑμῶν τὸ ἑαυτοῦ σκεῦος κτᾶσθαι ἐν ἁγιασμῷ καὶ τιμῇ,

      that each one of you know how to control his own body in sanctification and honor,

      Read 1 Thessalonians 4:4 in the app

Abbott-Smith dictionary entry (quoted)

7 κτάομαι, -ῶμαι, [in LXX chiefly for קנה;] in pres., impf., fut. and aor., to procure for oneself, get, gain, acquire (the pf. and plpf., to have acquired, hence to possess, do not occur in NT): c. acc. rei, Mt 10:9, Lk 18:12, Ac 8:20; c. gen. pret., Ac 22:28; ἐκ c. gen. pret., Ac 1:18; τ. ψυχὰς ὑμῶν (MM, xvi), Lk 21:19; τ. ἑαυτοῦ σκεῦος κτᾶσθαι, I Th 4:4 (where if σ. = body, κ. must = pf., κέκτημαι; v. MM, xvi; M, Th., in l.; Field, Notes, 72 f. But σ. is most freq. taken as = wife; v. Thayer, s.v.; Lft., Notes, 53 ff.; ICC, in l.).†
