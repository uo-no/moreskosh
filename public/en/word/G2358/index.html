Senses (NGSD)

  1. to lead in triumph (2)
    To include in a triumphal procession, to display a victory. It is not decided whether 2 Corinthians 2:14 is to be read as “leading in a triumphal procession” or as “causing to triumph”, nor how the subject of Colossians 2:15 is to be read.
    Attested: NT only

    • 2 Corinthians 2:14

      Τῷ δὲ θεῷ χάρις τῷ πάντοτε θριαμβεύοντι ἡμᾶς ἐν τῷ Χριστῷ καὶ τὴν ὀσμὴν τῆς γνώσεως αὐτοῦ φανεροῦντι δι’ ἡμῶν ἐν παντὶ τόπῳ·

      Now thanks be to God who always leads us in triumph in Christ, and reveals through us the sweet aroma of his knowledge in every place.

      Having stripped the principalities and the powers, he made a show of them openly, triumphing over them in it.

Abbott-Smith dictionary entry (quoted)

2 *† θριαμβεύω (< θρίαμβος, 1. a festal hymn to Bacchus. 2. The Roman triumphus), to triumph (and rarely, c. acc., to triumph over; so perh. Col 2:15, but v. infr.). to lead in triumph: c. acc. pers., II Co 2:14; hence, generally, to make a spectacle or show of: Col 2:15 (but v. supr.; cf. MM, Exp., xv; and esp. Field, Notes, 181).†
