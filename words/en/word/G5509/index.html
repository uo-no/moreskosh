Senses (NGSD)

  1. tunic / undergarment (11)
    The undergarment worn next to the skin, a tunic: “tunic and cloak”, “if anyone takes your cloak, give your tunic too”, “do not take two tunics”, “the tunic was seamless, woven in one piece”, “tunics and garments that Dorcas made”, “hating even the garment”, “tore his clothes”.
    Attested: NT · LXX · Fathers

    • Matthew 5:40

      καὶ τῷ θέλοντί σοι κριθῆναι καὶ τὸν χιτῶνά σου λαβεῖν, ἄφες αὐτῷ καὶ τὸ ἱμάτιον·

      If anyone sues you to take away your coat, let him have your cloak also.

      The high priest tore his clothes and said, “What further need have we of witnesses?

Abbott-Smith dictionary entry (quoted)

11 χιτών, ῶνος, ὁ [in LXX chiefly for כְּתֹנֶת:] the garment worn next the skin (though two tunics were sometimes worn, v. Swete, Mk., 117), a tunic: Mt 10:10, Mk 6:9 14:63, Lk 3:11 9:3, Ju 23; disting. from ἱμάτιον (q.v.), Mt 5:40, Lk 6:29, Jo 19:23, Ac 9:39 (v. DCG, i, 338a, 340a, 499a)
