Senses (NGSD)

  1. crown (of victor, of reward) (8)
    The crown of a winner in the games or in battle, the crown of reward given by God, a crown that is one's pride: “a perishable crown”, “an imperishable one”, “the crown of righteousness”, “the crown of life”, “my crown”.
    Attested: NT · LXX · Fathers

    • 1 Corinthians 9:25

      πᾶς δὲ ὁ ἀγωνιζόμενος πάντα ἐγκρατεύεται, ἐκεῖνοι μὲν οὖν ἵνα φθαρτὸν στέφανον λάβωσιν, ἡμεῖς δὲ ἄφθαρτον.

      Every man who strives in the games exercises self-control in all things. Now they do it to receive a corruptible crown, but we an incorruptible.

      For what is our hope, or joy, or crown of rejoicing? Isn’t it even you, before our Lord Jesus at his coming?

Abbott-Smith dictionary entry (quoted)

18 στέφανος, -ου, ὁ (< στέφω, to encircle), [in LXX chiefly for עֲטָרָה ;] that which surrounds or encompasses (as a wall, a crowd: Hom., al.). a crown, i.e. the wreath, garland or chaplet given as a prize for victory, as a festal ornament, or as a public honour for disting­uished service or personal worth (so to sovereigns, especially on the occasion of a παρουσία, q.v.; cf. Deiss., LAE, 372 ff.; on its use in LXX for the golden crown of royalty (prop., διάδημα, q.v., cf. Hort and Mayor on Ja 1:12): Mt 27:29, Mk 15:17, Jo 19:2, 5, I Co 9:25, Re 4:4, 10 6:2 9:7 12:1 14:14. Metaph.: Phl 4:1, Re 3:11; τ. δικαιοσύνης (cf. Deiss., LAE, 312), II Ti 4:8; τ. δόξης, I Pe 5:4; καυχήσεως, I Th 2:19 (v. M, Th., in l.); τ. ζωῆς (gen. appos.), Ja 1:12, Re 2:10.†
