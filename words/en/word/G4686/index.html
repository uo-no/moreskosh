Senses (NGSD)

  1. cohort, detachment of soldiers (7)
    A unit of the Roman army, a cohort: “a unit called the Italian Cohort”, “to the whole cohort”, “a centurion of the Augustan Cohort”, “taking the cohort with him”, “the cohort and the tribune”, “called together the whole cohort”, “gathered the whole cohort”.
    Attested: NT · LXX

      Then the governor’s soldiers took Jesus into the Praetorium, and gathered the whole garrison together against him.

Abbott-Smith dictionary entry (quoted)

7 ** σπείρα, -ης (on this form of gen., v. M, Pr., 38, 48; Bl., § 7, 1; Mayser, 12; Deiss., BS, 186), [in LXX: Jth 14:11, II Mac 8:23 Mac 12:20, 22 * ;] (= Lat. spira) anything or rolled up, a coil. As a military term used (by Polyb. and later writers) of a body of soldiers, a maniple (third part of a cohort) or cohort (v. Swete, Mk., 375; Westc., Jo., 251 f.): Mt 27:27, Mk 15:16, Jo 18:3, 12, Ac 10:1 21:31 27:1.†
