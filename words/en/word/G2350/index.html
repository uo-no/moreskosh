Senses (NGSD)

  1. to be in uproar, to disturb (4)
    To make a commotion, to throw into uproar, to be agitated: “threw the city into an uproar”, “do not be alarmed”, “the noisy crowd”, “why are you making a commotion and weeping”.
    Attested: NT · LXX

      When Jesus came into the ruler’s house and saw the flute players and the crowd in noisy disorder,

Abbott-Smith dictionary entry (quoted)

4 θορυβέω, -ῶ (< θόρυβος), [in LXX: Na 2:3(4) (רעל ho.), Da LXX 8:17 (בּעת ni.), Jg 3:26, Wi 18:19, Si 40:6*;] to make a noise or uproar: mid., of loud and ostentatious lamentation, Mt 9:23, Mk 5:39, Ac 20:10. Trans., to trouble, throw into confusion: τ. πόλιν, Ac 17:5.†
