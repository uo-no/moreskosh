Senses (NGSD)

  1. foreigner, barbarian (6)
    A foreigner who does not speak Greek; a barbarian: “I shall be a foreigner to the speaker”, “the speaker will be a foreigner to me”, “the local people showed us kindness”, “when the local people saw …”, “barbarian, Scythian”, “both to Greeks and to barbarians”.
    Attested: NT · LXX

    • Acts 28:2

      οἵ τε βάρβαροι παρεῖχον οὐ τὴν τυχοῦσαν φιλανθρωπίαν ἡμῖν, ἅψαντες γὰρ πυρὰν προσελάβοντο πάντας ἡμᾶς διὰ τὸν ὑετὸν τὸν ἐφεστῶτα καὶ διὰ τὸ ψῦχος.

      The natives showed us uncommon kindness; for they kindled a fire and received us all, because of the present rain and because of the cold.

Abbott-Smith dictionary entry (quoted)

6 βάρβαρος, -ον (prob. onomatop., descriptive of unintelligible sounds), [in LXX: Ps 113 (114):1 (לעז), Ez 21:31 (36) (בּער), II Mac 2:21 4:25 10:4, III Mac 3:24*;] barbarous, barbarian, strange to Greek language and culture (and also, after the Persian war, with the added sense of brutal, rude): Ac 28:2, 4, Ro 1:14, I Co 14:11, Col 3:11 (v. Lft., in l., and Notes, 249).†
