Senses (NGSD)

  1. to take one's place / recline / sit down (10)
    To take a place at a meal, to recline, to sit on the ground: “washed their feet and reclined”, “the people sat down”, “take the lowest place”, “reclined at table”, “come at once and sit down to eat”, “directed the crowd to sit down on the ground”.
    Attested: NT · LXX

    • Matthew 15:35

      καὶ παραγγείλας τῷ ὄχλῳ ἀναπεσεῖν ἐπὶ τὴν γῆν

      He commanded the multitude to sit down on the ground;

      Now as he spoke, a certain Pharisee asked him to dine with him. He went in and sat at the table.

      Read Luke 11:37 in the app

      When the hour had come, he sat down with the twelve apostles.

      Read Luke 22:14 in the app

  2. to lean back (2)
    To lean against, to lean back: “leaning back on Jesus' breast”.
    Attested: NT only

    • John 13:25

      He, leaning back, as he was, on Jesus’ chest, asked him, “Lord, who is it?”

Abbott-Smith dictionary entry (quoted)

12 ἀνα-πίπτω, [in LXX: Ge 49:9 (כּרע) To 2:1 7:8, Jth 12:10, Si 25:18 35 (32):2, Da TH Sus 37*;] (cl.) to fall back. In late writers = ἀνα-κλίνομαι, to recline for a repast (MM, VGT, s.v.): at table, Lk 11:37 14:10 17:7 22:14, Jo 13:12 21:20; on the ground, Mt 15:35, Mk 6:40 8:6, Jo 6:10; to lean back, Jo 13:25 (T, ἐπιπεσών; v.s. ἀνάκειμαι, ad fin.).† SYN.: ἀνάκειμαι (q.v.), ἀνακλίνομαι.
