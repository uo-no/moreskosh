Senses (NGSD)

  1. to stumble (5)
    To stumble, to commit a fault: “you will never stumble”, “we all stumble in many ways”, “one who does not stumble in what he says”, “one who fails in one point”, “they did not stumble so as to fall”.
    Attested: NT · LXX

    • Romans 11:11

      Λέγω οὖν, μὴ ἔπταισαν ἵνα πέσωσιν; μὴ γένοιτο· ἀλλὰ τῷ αὐτῶν παραπτώματι ἡ σωτηρία τοῖς ἔθνεσιν, εἰς τὸ παραζηλῶσαι αὐτούς.

      I ask then, did they stumble that they might fall? May it never be! But by their fall salvation has come to the Gentiles, to provoke them to jealousy.

      Read Romans 11:11 in the app

    • James 2:10

      ὅστις γὰρ ὅλον τὸν νόμον τηρήσῃ, πταίσῃ δὲ ἐν ἑνί, γέγονεν πάντων ἔνοχος.

      For whoever keeps the whole law, and yet stumbles in one point, he has become guilty of all.

      Read James 2:10 in the app

    • James 3:2

      πολλὰ γὰρ πταίομεν ἅπαντες. εἴ τις ἐν λόγῳ οὐ πταίει, οὗτος τέλειος ἀνήρ, δυνατὸς χαλιναγωγῆσαι καὶ ὅλον τὸ σῶμα.

      For we all stumble in many things. Anyone who doesn’t stumble in word is a perfect person, able to bridle the whole body also.

Abbott-Smith dictionary entry (quoted)

5 πταίω, [in LXX chiefly for נגף ni.;] trans., to cause to stumble (I Ki 4:3, cf. Deiss., BS, 681). Intrans., to stumble. Metaph., in moral sense, Ro 11:11, Ja 2:10 3:2, II Pe 1:10.†
