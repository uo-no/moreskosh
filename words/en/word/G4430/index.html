Senses (NGSD)

  1. corpse, dead body (7)
    A corpse, a dead body: “the disciples came, took the body away and buried it”, “wherever the corpse is”, “granted the body to Joseph”, “John’s disciples … came and took the body”, “their bodies … will lie exposed in the street of the great city”, “for three and a half days they gaze at the bodies”.
    Attested: NT · LXX

    • Matthew 14:12

      καὶ προσελθόντες οἱ μαθηταὶ αὐτοῦ ἦραν τὸ πτῶμα καὶ ἔθαψαν αὐτόν, καὶ ἐλθόντες ἀπήγγειλαν τῷ Ἰησοῦ.

      His disciples came, took the body, and buried it. Then they went and told Jesus.

    • Mark 6:29

      καὶ ἀκούσαντες οἱ μαθηταὶ αὐτοῦ ἦλθον καὶ ἦραν τὸ πτῶμα αὐτοῦ καὶ ἔθηκαν αὐτὸ ἐν μνημείῳ.

      When his disciples heard this, they came and took up his corpse and laid it in a tomb.

      Read Mark 6:29 in the app

    • Revelation 11:8

      καὶ τὸ πτῶμα αὐτῶν ἐπὶ τῆς πλατείας τῆς πόλεως τῆς μεγάλης, ἥτις καλεῖται πνευματικῶς Σόδομα καὶ Αἴγυπτος, ὅπου καὶ ὁ κύριος αὐτῶν ἐσταυρώθη.

      Their dead bodies will be in the street of the great city, which spiritually is called Sodom and Egypt, where also their Lord was crucified.

Abbott-Smith dictionary entry (quoted)

7 πτῶμα, -τος, τό (< πίπτω), [in LXX: Jg 14:8 (מַפֶּלֶת), Jb 16:15 (14) (פֶּרֶץ), Is 51:19 (שֹׁד), Jth 8:19, Wi 4:18, al.;] a fall, metaph., a misfortune, calamity (Trag., Plat., Polyb., al.; LXX). That which has fallen; of buildings, a ruin (Polyb.); of living creatures, in cl. (poët. only) usually c. gen., νεκρῶν, etc., but also absol., as in late writers and NT, a fallen body, a carcase, corpse: Mt 14:12 24:28, Mk 15:45; π. αὐτοῦ, Mk 6:29; αὐτῶν, Re 11:8, 9 (cf. Rutherford, NPhr., 472 f.).†
