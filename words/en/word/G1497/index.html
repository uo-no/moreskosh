Senses (NGSD)

  1. idol (11)
    An idol, an image of a god: “an idol has no real existence in the world”, “food offered to idols”, “mute idols”, “keep yourselves from idols”, “idols that govern the body”.
    Attested: NT · LXX · Fathers

    • Acts 7:41

      καὶ ἐμοσχοποίησαν ἐν ταῖς ἡμέραις ἐκείναις καὶ ἀνήγαγον θυσίαν τῷ εἰδώλῳ, καὶ εὐφραίνοντο ἐν τοῖς ἔργοις τῶν χειρῶν αὐτῶν.

      They made a calf in those days, and brought a sacrifice to the idol, and rejoiced in the works of their hands.

    • 1 Corinthians 8:4

      Περὶ τῆς βρώσεως οὖν τῶν εἰδωλοθύτων οἴδαμεν ὅτι οὐδὲν εἴδωλον ἐν κόσμῳ, καὶ ὅτι οὐδεὶς θεὸς εἰ μὴ εἷς.

      Therefore concerning the eating of things sacrificed to idols, we know that no idol is anything in the world, and that there is no other God but one.

      Read 1 Corinthians 8:4 in the app

    • 1 Corinthians 12:2

      οἴδατε ὅτι ὅτε ἔθνη ἦτε πρὸς τὰ εἴδωλα τὰ ἄφωνα ὡς ἂν ἤγεσθε ἀπαγόμενοι.

      You know that when you were heathen, you were led away to those mute idols, however you might be led.

Abbott-Smith dictionary entry (quoted)

11 εἴδωλον, -ου, τό (< εἶδος), [in LXX for אֱלֹוהָּ, גְּלוּלִים, etc. ;] in cl. a phantom, image, likeness; an image in the mind, an idea, fancy. In LXX and NT, an image of a god, an idol (cf. Polyb., xxxi, 3, 13): Ac 7:41, I Co 12:2, Re 9:20; the false god or idol worshipped in an image (ICC, on I Th, l.c.): Ac 15:20, Ro 2:22, I Co 8:4, 7 10:19 II Co 6:18, I Th 1:9, I Jo 5:21.†
