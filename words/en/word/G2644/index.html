Senses (NGSD)

  1. to reconcile, to be reconciled (6)
    To reconcile those who were at enmity (passive: to be reconciled). “be reconciled to her husband”, “reconciled us to God through Christ”, “in Christ God was reconciling the world to himself”, “be reconciled to God”, “while we were enemies we were reconciled to God”, “having been reconciled”.
    Attested: NT · LXX

    • Romans 5:10

      εἰ γὰρ ἐχθροὶ ὄντες κατηλλάγημεν τῷ θεῷ διὰ τοῦ θανάτου τοῦ υἱοῦ αὐτοῦ, πολλῷ μᾶλλον καταλλαγέντες σωθησόμεθα ἐν τῇ ζωῇ αὐτοῦ·

      For if while we were enemies, we were reconciled to God through the death of his Son, much more, being reconciled, we will be saved by his life.

    • 1 Corinthians 7:11

      ἐὰν δὲ καὶ χωρισθῇ, μενέτω ἄγαμος ἢ τῷ ἀνδρὶ καταλλαγήτω καὶ ἄνδρα γυναῖκα μὴ ἀφιέναι.

      (but if she departs, let her remain unmarried, or else be reconciled to her husband), and that the husband not leave his wife.

      Read 1 Corinthians 7:11 in the app

    • 2 Corinthians 5:19

      ὡς ὅτι θεὸς ἦν ἐν Χριστῷ κόσμον καταλλάσσων ἑαυτῷ, μὴ λογιζόμενος αὐτοῖς τὰ παραπτώματα αὐτῶν, καὶ θέμενος ἐν ἡμῖν τὸν λόγον τῆς καταλλαγῆς.

      namely, that God was in Christ reconciling the world to himself, not reckoning to them their trespasses, and having committed to us the word of reconciliation.

Abbott-Smith dictionary entry (quoted)

6 κατ-αλλάσσω (Att. -ττω; cf. ἀλλάσσω), [in LXX: Je 31 (48):39 (חתת), II Mac 1:5 7:33 8:29*;] prop., to change, exchange (esp. of money); hence, of persons, to change from emnity to friendship, to reconcile (for exx. in cl., v. Thayer, LS): of the reconciliation of man to God (Lft., Notes, 288; ICC on Ro 5:10; DCG, ii, 474, 797), II Co 5:18, 19; pass., Ro 5:10, II Co 5:20; of a woman returning to her husband, I Co 7:11 (cf. ἀπο-καταλλάσσω).†
