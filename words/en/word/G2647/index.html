Senses (NGSD)

  1. to destroy / tear down (11)
    To demolish a building, temple, or dwelling, to pull it down until not one stone is left on another: “destroy the temple and rebuild it in three days”, “tear down what one built”, “do not destroy the work of God”.
    Attested: NT only

    • Matthew 24:2

      ὁ δὲ ἀποκριθεὶς εἶπεν αὐτοῖς· Οὐ βλέπετε ταῦτα πάντα; ἀμὴν λέγω ὑμῖν, οὐ μὴ ἀφεθῇ ὧδε λίθος ἐπὶ λίθον ὃς οὐ καταλυθήσεται.

      But he answered them, “You see all of these things, don’t you? Most certainly I tell you, there will not be left here one stone on another, that will not be thrown down.”

      Jesus said to him, “Do you see these great buildings? There will not be left here one stone on another, which will not be thrown down.”

  2. to abolish / annul / overthrow (4)
    To abolish, annul, or overthrow the law, the prophets, a plan, or a movement: “do not think I have come to abolish the Law or the Prophets”, “if it is of God, you will not be able to overthrow them”.

      “Don’t think that I came to destroy the law or the prophets. I didn’t come to destroy, but to fulfill.

  3. to lodge / find lodging (2)
    A traveller stops at an inn or another's house and lodges.

Abbott-Smith dictionary entry (quoted)

17 κατα-λύω, [in LXX for לוּן, שׁבת, etc.;] to destroy, cast down: Mt 24:2, Mk 13:2, Lk 21:6; τ. ναόν, Mt 26:61 27:40, Mk 14:58 15:29, Ac 6:14; οἰκίαν, II Co 5:1; opp. to οἰκοδομεῖν, Ga 2:18. Metaph., to overthrow, annul, abrogate: Ac 5:38, 39; τ. ἔργον τ. θεοῦ, Ro 14:20; τ. νόμον, Mt 5:17 (II Mac 2:22). to unloose, unyoke (e.g. horses), hence intrans., of travellers (cf. κατάλυμα), to take up one's quarters, lodge (cl., Ge 19:2, al.): Lk 9:12 19:7.†
