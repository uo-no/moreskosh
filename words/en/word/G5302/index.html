Senses (NGSD)

  1. to lack / be short of (9)
    What is needed is not enough, is missing, is scarce: “what do I still lack?”, “one thing you still lack”, “began to be in need”.
    Attested: NT · LXX

    • Matthew 19:20

      λέγει αὐτῷ ὁ νεανίσκος· Πάντα ταῦτα ἐφύλαξα· τί ἔτι ὑστερῶ;

      The young man said to him, “All these things I have observed from my youth. What do I still lack?”

      Read Matthew 19:20 in the app

    • Luke 22:35

      Καὶ εἶπεν αὐτοῖς· Ὅτε ἀπέστειλα ὑμᾶς ἄτερ βαλλαντίου καὶ πήρας καὶ ὑποδημάτων, μή τινος ὑστερήσατε; οἱ δὲ εἶπαν· Οὐθενός.

      He said to them, “When I sent you out without purse, bag, and sandals, did you lack anything?” They said, “Nothing.”

      Read Luke 22:35 in the app

    • 2 Corinthians 11:9

      καὶ παρὼν πρὸς ὑμᾶς καὶ ὑστερηθεὶς οὐ κατενάρκησα οὐθενός· τὸ γὰρ ὑστέρημά μου προσανεπλήρωσαν οἱ ἀδελφοὶ ἐλθόντες ἀπὸ Μακεδονίας· καὶ ἐν παντὶ ἀβαρῆ ἐμαυτὸν ὑμῖν ἐτήρησα καὶ τηρήσω.

      When I was present with you and was in need, I wasn’t a burden on anyone, for the brothers, when they came from Macedonia, supplied the measure of my need. In everything I kept myself from being burdensome to you, and I will continue to do so.

      Read 2 Corinthians 11:9 in the app

  2. to fall short / be inferior (6)
    Not to reach a standard, to be inferior: “fall short of the glory of God”, “fail to obtain the grace of God”, “not inferior”.
    Attested: NT · LXX

    • Romans 3:23

      πάντες γὰρ ἥμαρτον καὶ ὑστεροῦνται τῆς δόξης τοῦ θεοῦ,

      for all have sinned, and fall short of the glory of God;

      Read Romans 3:23 in the app

    • 2 Corinthians 11:5

      λογίζομαι γὰρ μηδὲν ὑστερηκέναι τῶν ὑπερλίαν ἀποστόλων·

      For I reckon that I am not at all behind the very best apostles.

      Read 2 Corinthians 11:5 in the app

    • Hebrews 4:1

      Φοβηθῶμεν οὖν μήποτε καταλειπομένης ἐπαγγελίας εἰσελθεῖν εἰς τὴν κατάπαυσιν αὐτοῦ δοκῇ τις ἐξ ὑμῶν ὑστερηκέναι·

      Let’s fear therefore, lest perhaps anyone of you should seem to have come short of a promise of entering into his rest.

      Read Hebrews 4:1 in the app

  3. to run out (supply) (1)
    “When the wine ran out” (John 2:3).
    Attested: NT only

    • John 2:3

      καὶ ὑστερήσαντος οἴνου λέγει ἡ μήτηρ τοῦ Ἰησοῦ πρὸς αὐτόν· Οἶνον οὐκ ἔχουσιν.

      When the wine ran out, Jesus’ mother said to him, “They have no wine.”

Abbott-Smith dictionary entry (quoted)

16 ὑστερέω, -ῶ (< ὕστερος), [in LXX for חָסֵר, חָדַל etc. ;] to come late, be behind (opp. to προτερέω, φθάνω; c. gen. rei, for; c. gen. pers., later than). Metaph., of persons, absol., to come short, fail: He 4:1; seq. ἀπό, 12:15.; c. gen. pers., to come short of, be inferior to: II Co 11:5; οὐδέν (in nothing, in no respect), ib. 12:11; with reference to things, to come short (of), be in want (of): c. acc rei, Mt 19:20 (Si 51:24); c. gen. rei, Lk 22:35; so mid. (Diod., FlJ), Ro 3:23; absol., to be in want, suffer want, Lk 15:14, I Co 8:8, II Co 11:8, He 11:37 (Si 11:11); opp. to περισσεύειν, Phl 4:12; seq. ἐν, I Co 1:7. Of things, to fail, be lacking: Jo 2:3; c. acc pers. (v. Swete, in l.; Mozley, Ps., 42), Mk 10:21; to be inferior: mid., I Co 12:24 (cf. ἀφ-υστερέω).†
